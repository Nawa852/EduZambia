 1. What the Parent’s World Solves

 end, WhatsApp rumors replace facts, and the technology their children use every day is a closed
 door. The Parent’s World fixes all three:

 3. Page One — Home (Today)
   • All children, one screen: today’s verified study time, pending assignments, upcoming tests —
   switch between children in one tap.
   • Tonight’s conversation starter: "ask Chipo about osmosis — she practised it today" —
   matched to the actual syllabus, so any parent can help, no degree required.
   • Priority alerts only: missed work, slipping mastery, absence — urgent things first, everything
   else in the Alerts page.
   • School announcements and unread teacher messages.


 4. Page Two — Progress (the Truth, Live)
   • Mastery per subject and competence: "mastered 8 of 11 competences in Mathematics this
   term" — not a single mark, but the full picture.
   • Trends term over term: improving, steady, or slipping — with the moment the change began.
   • Class-average comparison: context without competition — is my child keeping pace?
   • Exam-readiness: Synapse’s estimate per subject, and what would raise it fastest.
   • Career lens: what this term’s competences unlock — nursing, engineering, accounting — so
   progress feels like direction.


 5. Page Three — Study Reports (Verified)
   • Session evidence: "Elena studied 32 minutes today — flashcards and 12 algebra questions,
   engaged, verified".
   • Focus-time history: the week’s study pattern at a glance — streaks, gaps, best hours.
   • Optional learning confirmation (opt-in, parent-controlled): on-device verification that genuine
   engagement happened. No raw images — only the verified signal leaves the device.
   • Notifications when it matters: no study for two days, a broken streak, a late-night session —
   the parent can act the same week, not at report time.

 6. Page Four — Documents
   • Report cards as published: school-branded PDFs arrive in the inbox the moment the school
   releases them — signed, sealed, archived.
   • Test results and certificates: every marked assessment and achievement certificate, kept
   forever — no lost papers.
   • Term summaries: a plain-language wrap-up per child, readable in two minutes, shareable with
   family.


 7. Page Five — Connect (Teachers & School)
   • Direct messaging: logged conversations with teachers and the school — read receipts, no
   phone-call tag, no lost WhatsApp threads.
   • Meeting booking: teachers publish available slots; parents book in two taps; both sides get
   reminders.
   • Announcements feed: the official channel — what the school says, not what the group chat
   heard.
   • Two-way respect: teachers send canned updates in seconds ("your child mastered 4.1.4 this
   week"), parents reply in context.


 8. Page Six — Home Support
   • Conversation starters: matched to this week’s actual syllabus — "tonight, ask about the water
   cycle" turns dinner into revision.
   • Plain-language guides: every topic explained for adults in two-minute reads and audio — in
   English and major Zambian languages.
   • Routine builder: a gentle shared study schedule the parent and child agree on; Synapse
   nudges both sides.
   • Celebration cues: when the child masters a competence, the parent gets the good news and a
   suggestion to acknowledge it — recognition drives motivation.

 The feature no education platform offers: the parents themselves learn. Short, practical, in plain
 language and local languages — because a family that understands the technology raises a child

 9.2 How It Works
   • One lesson a week by default — five minutes, audio-first (works hands-free and low-literacy
   friendly), downloadable for offline.
   • In English, Bemba, Nyanja and more; large text; no jargon.
   • Synapses with the child’s world: when Chipo starts learning web design, the parent gets "what
   web design is and why it matters" — the Academy explains what the child is doing, exactly
   when it becomes relevant.
   • Certificates for completed tracks — celebrated in the family feed; some schools may
   recognize parent-graduates at open days.
   • Ask Synapse: a plain-language Q&A; — "what is artificial intelligence?" — answered without
   jargon, cite-checked, in the parent’s language.


 10. Pages Eight & Nine — Alerts, and Me
   • Alerts: one inbox for everything urgent — absence, missed assignments, slipping mastery,
   school notices — tuned by the parent (quiet hours, per-child settings).
   • Me: multi-child management (one account, many children), language preference, notification
   controls, privacy settings (what the parent shares, what stays private), guardian-link
   management, offline downloads.

 12. Trust & Privacy
   • Guardians see only their own linked children — enforced at the database level, not just the
   interface.
   • Monitoring features are opt-in and parent-controlled; on-device processing for anything
   sensitive; nothing covert, ever.
   • Schools see aggregates, not parent conversations; teachers see only their classes’ families.



 13. First Build (MVP Slice)

   • Later phases: meeting booking, routine builder, remaining Academy tracks, USSD result
   checks for parents without smartphones.


 14. Why This Matters
   • An informed parent is the cheapest, most powerful intervention in education. Synapse makes
   every parent informed — and then makes them more capable still.
   • The family that learns together no longer fears the technology the child carries in their
   pocket. That is the Synapse difference.
